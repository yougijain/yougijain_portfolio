Sep 2026 Live Real-Time Computer Vision

Hall Check

Counts the people in line at four UMass dining halls from their public camera streams, one frame every two minutes, live since October 26. YOLO11n runs on a CPU and counts people whose feet fall inside a queue polygon. Ultralytics shrinks the 1280×720 streams to 640 by default, which loses the people at the far counters, so I run it at full size. On the same Berkshire frame it found 21 people where 640 found 7, at 163 ms a frame instead of 56 and 522 MiB peak memory instead of 433. The CPU-only PyTorch wheel cut the worker image by more than a third, and CI fails if a CUDA library comes back. Frames are never stored: CI also fails on any pixel-writing call. The accuracy report is fixed in advance and refuses to produce a number until 150+ blind hand counts cover every hall, meal, and lighting condition.

2025–2026 Live ML / Calibrated Risk Model

NYC Collisions

A gradient-boosted model estimates how likely each crash was to injure someone from what was involved but not where it happened. On 121,970 held-out 2025–26 crashes it scores ROC-AUC 0.79 against 0.54 for a borough-and-hour base rate. Five rolling-forward fits score 413,780 crashes so none is scored by a model that trained on it. Of 1,093 ranked intersections, 101 clear z = 1.96 where chance predicts about 27, but only 2 survive Benjamini–Hochberg, so the list is a screening queue. A backtest shows the ranking persists, though it only ties a no-model sort. A weekly job rebuilds all 637,256 crashes behind 386 tests.

Oct 2026 Power Analysis / Multiple Testing

When Evals Can't Tell

How large an LLM eval has to be before a 5-point gap between two prompts means anything. On 100 items, the usual two-proportion test catches a real 80% vs. 85% gap 15% of the time, and 80% power takes 903 items. Both systems answer the same items, so McNemar's paired test applies, and when they disagree on 10% of items it needs 312. Score 50 prompts that are no better than baseline on 300 items, keep the best, and it averages +6.5 points from selection alone. The CLI prints what an eval could detect before any p-value, and CI fails if the regenerated results drift from the committed ones.

Oct 2026 NLP / Transformers

Text vs. Timing

Does posting time predict engagement beyond what a post says? Three text models run with and without the timestamp on the same split: TF-IDF with logistic regression, frozen MiniLM embeddings under the same linear head, and BERT fine-tuned with a temporal fusion head on a Colab T4. The frozen row separates what pretrained semantics buy from what fine-tuning buys. On 20,000 Stack Overflow posts, no model beats the majority class by more than 1.4 points and fine-tuned BERT ties TF-IDF. Adding the timestamp to TF-IDF fixes 34 predictions and breaks 32 (McNemar p = 0.90), and the generated report calls that inconclusive rather than a gain.

Jun 2026 Experimentation / Causal

When Average Effects Lie

Turns a 64,000-customer randomized email test into a targeting plan. The Women's email's +4.5-point visit lift splits into +7.3 for past buyers of women's merchandise and +1.1 for everyone else, with Benjamini–Hochberg control across the interaction tests. I picked among T-, S-, and DR-learners by 5-fold cross-fitted Qini on training rows. Picking on the reporting split would have quoted a men's-email Qini of 19.3 instead of 2.6. At cost break-even, targeting the Women's email adds $16.95 per 1,000 customers (95% CI $2.99 to $30.60) and sends 415 fewer emails. The Men's email goes to everyone, because its targeting gain can't be told from zero.
